5      Citing Ertl v. Ertl, 871 N.W.2d 410 (Minn. App. 2015), husband asserts that the added provisions to the signed QDRO

       persuaded. Unlike Ertl—where the district court sua sponte modified substantive terms—wife filed a motion attaching
       the proposed QDRO two weeks prior to the hearing and, as explained above, the proposed QDRO did not amend any
       substantive terms.

6      Husband makes various arguments that wife's motion did not comply with the Minnesota General Rules of Practice. But

       or de minimis, see Minn. R. Civ. P. 61 (requiring courts to ignore harmless error); Hesse v. Hesse, 778 N.W.2d 98, 105
       (Minn. App. 2009) (noting that appellate courts ignore prejudicial error when prejudice is de minimis). Even if wife had

       any error is harmless.
