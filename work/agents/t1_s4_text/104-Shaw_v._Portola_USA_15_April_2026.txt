 4                  Finally, and most troubling, Plaintiff cites Boyd v. City of San Diego, 14

 5   Cal.App.4th 954, 959-960 (1993) five separate times. See ECF No. 23 pgs. 4, 5, 7, 8, and 10. The

 6   Court not found any such case and therefore finds Plaintiff has cited a nonexistent case. 6 Plaintiff

 7   fails to address Boyd in her reply despite Defendants directing Plaintiff’s attention to the error,

 8   ECF No. 24, pg. 4. Plaintiff instead asserts that “Plaintiff has not cited fictitious cases and there is

 9   no basis to infer bad faith from correctable citation defects.” ECF No. 26, pg. 12.

10                  The Court agrees that any correctable citation defects do not necessarily indicate

11   bad faith nor warrant sanctions, as previously addressed herein. However, Plaintiff declined to

12   address the fictitious citation to Boyd despite Defendants raising the issue. Plaintiff had the

13   opportunity to make the necessary corrections to her Boyd citation or take accountability for

14   citing a nonexistent case, but instead claimed she “has not cited fictitious cases.” Thus, the

15   undersigned finds that, not only did Plaintiff cite a “fictitious,” or nonexistent case, but Plaintiff

16   also made a false representation to the Court by claiming that she did not cite any fictitious cases.

17                  Accordingly, Plaintiff is directed to show cause in writing within 14 days of this

18   order why sanctions should not be imposed for citing a nonexistent case in ECF No. 23.

19                  IT IS SO ORDERED.
