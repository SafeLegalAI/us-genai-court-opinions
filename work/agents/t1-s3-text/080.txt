 6   II.     DISCUSSION

 7           On January 13, 2026, the Court ordered Petitioner’s counsel to order the transcript from

 8   the January 13, 2026 hearing where the hallucinated citations in Petitioner’s TRO reply brief was

 9   addressed, provide the transcript to law student Herrera and any staff who assist him, require the

10   law student and any staff who assist counsel to read the transcript, and provide written

11   confirmation to the Courtroom Deputy of compliance within two months of the January 13, 2026

12   hearing. (ECF No. 18) Counsel was also required to review his other immigration habeas cases

13   filed in this district to see if those cases also had hallucinated citation issues, and if so, to bring

14   those issues to the attention of the district court. At the April 17, 2026 OSC hearing, counsel and

15   the law student confirmed that they both receive CM/ECF notifications of all court filings,

16   including the January 13, 2026 minutes that summarized the Court’s order in writing.

17           Based on Petitioner’s OSC response, the April 17, 2026 OSC hearing, and supplemental

18   filings, the Court concludes that Petitioner’s counsel failed to fully comply with the Court’s

19   January 13, 2026 order. Counsel apparently had the law student attempt to comply with the

20   Court’s order; the law student ordered an audio recording of the hearing and stated at the hearing

21   that she listened to the audio recording. (See ECF No. 50.) Counsel and the law student

22   reviewed their other immigration habeas cases filed in this district and identified one other case

23   with hallucinated citations, and filed a Notice of Errata in this case. 4/15/2026 Robert Cummings

24   Decl. ¶¶ 10-11 (ECF No. 50 at 43); 4/15/2026 Gina Herrera Decl. ¶ 12 (ECF No. 50); Notice of

25   Errata (ECF No. 55.). Petitioner’s counsel failed to provide written confirmation of compliance,

26   failed to order the written transcript, and failed to require the law student to read the transcript.

27           Petitioner’s counsel acknowledged that he failed to adequately supervise the law student

28   where he did not adequately check her work, including various citations in the filings at issue. It
                                                          3
 1   is also unclear whether counsel even realized Ms. Herrera was using ChatGPT to assist in drafting

 3   be sincerely remorseful for his failures at the OSC hearing. See also 4/15/2026 Cummings Decl.

 4   (ECF No. 50). The Court also credits counsel’s explanation that during the time period when

 7   help care for his elderly, ill father, who passed away on February 25, 2026. 4/15/2026 Cummings

 9           At the OSC hearing, Ms. Herrera candidly responded to the Court’s questions, openly

10   acknowledging her use of ChatGPT to assist in drafting the filings at issue and to verify citations.

11   She also sincerely acknowledged her error and at least initially with the TRO reply brief,

12   appeared unaware that ChatGPT and other artificial intelligence (“AI”) and generative AI tools

13   could provide hallucinated or inaccurate information. Though the Court finds troubling that the

14   table of authorities provided in the OSC response (ECF No. 50) did not follow the Court’s

15   instructions where it incorrectly identified certain hallucinated citations as accurate 2 and did not

16   include the verbatim citation for certain citations identified as hallucinated, the Court accepts Ms.

17   Herrera’s explanation and concludes that these errors were not intended to mislead the Court.

18   These errors and the apparent lack of understanding regarding the failure to follow the Court’s

19   instructions underscores, however, the critical need for training before counsel, law students, and

20   parties use certain AI tools in court or court filings.

 7   III.    CONCLUSION

 8           After careful consideration, including the involvement of a law student at the beginning of

 9   their legal career and for the reasons stated above, the Court exercises its discretion to discharge

10   the OSC (ECF No. 49) and declines to impose sanctions on Petitioner’s counsel Cummings or

11   law student Herrera for counsel’s failure to fully comply with the Court’s January 13, 2026 order.

12   The Court repeats its admonishment that while AI and generative AI tools can assist counsel and

13   parties, these tools must be used responsibly. They should not be used to verify legal authority

14   and citations, especially when generative AI tools were used to generate or identify the legal

15   authority and citations being checked.
