MEMORANDUM OPINION
Appellant Manny Kourradi, proceeding pro se, appeals from the trial court’s
final judgment dated December 2, 2025. Because Kourradi has failed to file a
compliant appellant’s brief after two orders from this Court, we dismiss the appeal.

On February 3, 2026, Kourradi filed his appellate brief, which contained some
deficiencies. Before the Court could address those deficiencies, on February 4, 2026,
Kourradi filed a motion for leave to file an amended brief and simultaneously filed
a copy of the proposed amended brief. We granted leave to file the amended brief
yet, because the amended brief failed to comply with rule 38.1 of the Texas Rules of
Appellate Procedure, we struck Kourradi’s amended brief and ordered that he file
another appellant’s brief that corrected the deficiencies. In that order, we notified
Kourradi that his brief failed to comply with rule 38.1(b), (g), and (i) of the Texas
Rules of Appellate Procedure.
One day after we issued our order, Kourradi filed a corrected brief. However,
Kourradi’s appellate brief still did not comply with the requirements of the Texas
Rules of Appellate Procedure. The brief failed to include (1) references to the record
in support of the statement of facts, (2) appropriate citations to authorities in support
of the argument, and (3) an appendix with the necessary contents, including a copy
of “the trial court’s judgment or other appealable order from which relief is sought.”
TEX. R. APP. P. 38.1(g), (i), (k). The brief also included incomplete legal citations
accompanied by unintelligible numeric strings (e.g., “2990836”). We again struck
Kourradi’s appellate brief and ordered Kourradi to file a corrected brief. We
admonished Kourradi to ensure that record references and citations to authorities are
accurate. See Campbell v. Campbell, No. 03-25-00388-CV, 2026 WL 179402, at *2

A civil litigant has the right to represent himself at trial and on appeal, but that
right carries with it the responsibility to adhere to the rules of evidence and
procedure, including our appellate procedural rules. Steele v. Humphreys, No. 0519-00988-CV, 2020 WL 6440499, at *2 (Tex. App.—Dallas Nov. 3, 2020, no pet.)
(mem. op.); Bolling, 315 S.W.3d at 895; see Mansfield State Bank v. Cohn, 573
S.W.2d 181, 184 (Tex. 1978) (“[N]o basis exists for differentiating between litigants
represented by counsel and litigants not represented by counsel in determining
whether rules of procedure must be followed.”). A pro se litigant is held to the same
standard as a licensed attorney and must comply with the Texas Rules of Appellate
Procedure. See Garrett v. Lee, No. 01-21-00498-CV, 2021 WL 5702177, at *2 (Tex.
App.—Houston [1st Dist.] Dec. 2, 2021, pet. denied) (mem. op.); Yeldell v. Denton
Cent. Appraisal Dist., No. 2-07-313-CV, 2008 WL 4053014, at *2 (Tex. App.—Fort
Worth Aug. 29, 2008, pet. denied) (mem. op.) (“[A]ll parties appearing in the
appellate courts of Texas must conform to the Texas Rules of Appellate
Procedure.”).
When, as here, an appellant is afforded the opportunity to refile a brief and
subsequently files a brief that again fails to comply with the Texas Rules of
Appellate Procedure, an appellate court may strike the brief, prohibit appellant from
6

filing another, and proceed as if the appellant failed to file a brief. See TEX. R. APP.
P. 38.9(a). When an appellant fails to file a brief, the Court may dismiss the appeal
for want of prosecution. TEX. R. APP. P. 38.8(a)(1); Carrero, 2025 WL 2956231, at
*2 (striking amended brief and dismissing appeal for want of prosecution where
appellant was ordered to file amended brief but amended brief still did not comply
with Texas Rules of Appellate Procedure).
Because Kourradi’s fourth brief, filed on July 17, 2026, fails to correct the
identified deficiencies and does not comply with rule 38.1, we strike his brief and
we dismiss the appeal for want of prosecution. See TEX. R. APP. P. 38.9(a), 42.3(b),
43.2(f); Carrero, 2025 WL 2956231, at *2. We dismiss any pending motions as
moot.
