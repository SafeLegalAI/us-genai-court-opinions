2

Jurisdiction is a threshold inquiry, and “[f]ederal courts are presumed to lack jurisdiction,

3

‘unless the contrary appears affirmatively from the record.’” Casey v. Lewis, 4 F.3d 1516,

4

1519 (9th Cir. 1993) (quoting Bender v. Williamsport Area Sch. Dist., 475 U.S. 534, 546

7

8

at 1380. A federal court’s jurisdiction may be established in one of two ways: actions

9

arising under federal law or those between citizens of different states in which the

10

alleged damages exceed $75,000. 28 U.S.C. §§ 1331, 1332. “Subject-matter jurisdiction

11

can never be waived or forfeited,” and “courts are obligated to consider sua sponte”

12

subject matter jurisdiction even when not raised by the parties. Gonzalez v. Thaler, 565

13

U.S. 134, 141 (2012).

B.

15

28 U.S.C. § 1441(a) states that “any civil action brought in a State court of which

16

the district courts of the United States have original jurisdiction, may be removed by the

17

defendant or the defendants, to the district court of the United States.” “The removal

18

statute is strictly construed against removal jurisdiction, and the burden of establishing

19

20

Dynegy, Inc., 375 F.3d 831, 838 (9th Cir. 2004), opinion amended on denial of reh'g, 387

22

28 U.S.C. § 1447(c).

C.

4

Pro se pleadings are to be liberally construed and afforded the benefit of any

5

doubt. Chambers v. Herrera, 78 F.4th 1100, 1104 (9th Cir. 2023). However, the court

6

need not accept as true conclusory allegations, unreasonable inferences, or

7

unwarranted deductions of fact. Western Mining Council v. Watt, 643 F.2d 618, 624 (9th

8

Cir. 1981). To give fair notice of the claims and the grounds on which they rest, a plaintiff

9

must allege with at least some degree of particularity overt acts by specific defendants

10

which support the claims. See Kimes v. Stone, 84 F.3d 1121, 1129 (9th Cir. 1996). A

11

formulaic recitation of the elements of a cause of action does not suffice to state a claim.

12

Bell Atlantic Corp. v. Twombly, 550 U.S. 544, 555-57 (2007); Ashcroft v. Iqbal, 556 U.S.

13

662, 678 (2009). To state a claim on which relief may be granted, the plaintiff must

14

allege enough facts “to state a claim to relief that is plausible on its face.” Twombly, 550

15

U.S. at 570. “A claim has facial plausibility when the plaintiff pleads factual content that

16

allows the court to draw the reasonable inference that the defendant is liable for the

17

misconduct alleged.” Iqbal, 556 U.S. at 678.

Rule 8, Pro Se Pleadings, Construction and Amendment

18

Upon dismissal of any claims, the court must tell a pro se plaintiff of a pleading’s

19

deficiencies and provide an opportunity to cure such defects. Garity v. APWU Nat'l Lab.

20

Org., 828 F.3d 848, 854 (9th Cir. 2016). However, if amendment would be futile, leave to

21

22

1243 (9th Cir. 2023).

To determine the propriety of a dismissal motion, the court may not consider facts

10

Here, Defendant points out that the FAC alleges $800,000,000 in punitive and

11

12

14

15

16

17

3.

Constructive Discharge and Wrongful Termination in Violation of
Public Policy

Defendant moves to dismiss Plaintiff’s causes of action for constructive discharge
because it is not an independent cause of action and wrongful termination in violation of
public policy where the FAC only restates the elements. Def. Mot. at 14-16. As a
preliminary matter, the Court agrees with Defendant that constructive discharge is not a
standalone cause of action, but rather “a doctrine that transforms what is ostensibly a
resignation into a firing.” Turner v. Anheuser-Busch, Inc., 7 Cal. 4th 1238, 1251 (1994).
Therefore, the Court recommends dismissing Plaintiff’s constructive discharge as an
independent cause of action.
To state a claim for wrongful termination in violation of public policy, Plaintiff must
allege “(1) an employer-employee relationship, (2) the employer terminated [Plaintiff’s]
employment, (3) the termination was substantially motivated by a violation of public
policy, and (4) the discharge caused [Plaintiff] harm.” Yau v. Santa Margarita Ford, Inc.,
229 Cal. App. 4th 144, 154 (2014). “Employment discrimination violates public policy and
will support a wrongful discrimination claim.” Galarpe v. United Airlines, Inc., 2018 WL
11

Case 2:26-cv-02007-DC-CSK

1

348161, at *3 (N.D. Cal. Jan. 10, 2018). “Whether Plaintiff has adequately pleaded a

2

wrongful termination claim is coincident of whether he has adequately pleaded a

violation of FEHA.” Id. Constructive discharge may be used to support a wrongful

4

discharge cause of action. See Starzynzki v. Capital Public Radio, Inc., 88 Cal. App. 4th

5

21

22

7

Upon dismissal of any claims, the court is to tell a pro se plaintiff of the

8

complaint’s deficiencies and provide an opportunity to cure such defects. Garity v.

9

10

11

12

Cato v. United States, 70 F.3d 1103, 1106 (9th Cir. 1995). However, if, after careful

13

consideration, it is clear that a claim cannot be cured by amendment, the court may

14

dismiss without leave to amend. Cato, 70 F.3d at 1105-06.

15

Here, Plaintiff should be given leave to amend because it is conceivable that the

16

defects in the Complaint can be cured and due to Plaintiff’s pro se status. Plaintiff is

17

granted leave to amend his Complaint to the extent he can allege facts that sufficiently

18

plead his claims. The allegations of the amended complaint must be set forth in

19

sequentially numbered paragraphs, with each paragraph number being one greater than

20

the one before, each paragraph having its own number, and no paragraph number being

21

repeated anywhere in the complaint. Each paragraph should be limited “to a single set of

22

circumstances” where possible. See Fed. R. Civ. P. 10(b). Forms are available to help

23

plaintiff organize his complaint in the proper way. They are available at the Clerk's

24

Office, 501 I Street, 4th Floor (Rm. 4-200), Sacramento, CA 95814, or online at

www.uscourts.gov/forms/pro-se-forms.
