                          MEMORANDUM OPINION AND ORDER

       This matter is before the Court on pro se Plaintiff Charles Reyes’s Motion for an Order to

Show Cause, Doc. 61, Defendants’ response, Doc. 66, and Mr. Reyes’s reply, Doc. 68. Mr.

Reyes seeks sanctions based on Defendants’ alleged pattern of misrepresentations to the Court.

See Doc. 61 at 3. Mr. Reyes also moves the Court to strike Defendants Bryce Bailey and Todd

Danley’s notice joining Defendants’ response, Doc. 70, as untimely. Doc. 71.

       The Court construes Mr. Reyes’s Motion for an Order to Show Cause, Doc. 61, as a

motion for sanctions under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 11, which “allows a district court to

award sanctions either sua sponte or on a party’s motion.” Mellott v. MSN Commc’ns, Inc., 492

Fed. Appx. 887, 888 (10th Cir. 2012). Relevant here, “Rule 11(b)(2) requires only that the

claims, defenses and other legal contentions in any documents filed with the Court are warranted

by existing law.” Bowman v. Friedman, 2022 WL 1091289, at *2 (D.N.M.). “Rule 11(b)(3)

requires only that any factual contentions in documents filed with the Court are warranted on the

evidence or will likely have evidentiary support after a reasonable opportunity for further

investigation or discovery.” Id. at *3.

       Mr. Reyes appears to argue that Defendants have violated Rule 11 by “misrepresenting

the record and the law.” Doc. 61 at 2. He contends that Defendants: (1) misrepresented that he

hallucinated a case citation in his pleadings; (2) “failed to comply with 28 U.S.C. § 1446(a) by

omitting critical state-court filings from the Notice of Removal”; (3) moved to quash the

summons issued for improper service based on false factual assertions; (4) asserted an

affirmative defense unsupported by existing law; and (5) failed to specifically controvert his

statement of undisputed facts in their response to his motions for summary judgment, as required

by Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 56. Doc. 61 at 3.

       The Court rejects Mr. Reyes’s arguments and denies the motion. First, Mr. Reyes admits

that he miscited a case in his Amended Complaint. See Doc. 65. Thus, Defendants’ contention

that Mr. Reyes may have fabricated a case was reasonable. Second, there is no indication that

Defendants’ failure to transmit the full state court record had any appreciable effect on the

litigation of this case. Third, there is no indication that Defendants filed the motion to quash for

improper service with the intent to delay or needlessly increase litigation. Fourth, the Court has

rejected Mr. Reyes’s argument, raised on summary judgment, that Defendants are collaterally

estopped from asserting a defense of good faith belief. See Doc. 85. As a result, the Court finds

no Rule 11 violation in the defense raised by Defendants. Fifth, Mr. Reyes misunderstands the

requirements of Rule 56. Mr. Reyes had the initial burden to allege particular undisputed facts

that entitled him to summary judgment as a matter of law. Mr. Reyes did not meet that burden.

Therefore, the burden never shifted to Defendants to designate specific facts showing the

existence of a genuine issue for trial. For these reasons, the Court declines to issue sanctions.
