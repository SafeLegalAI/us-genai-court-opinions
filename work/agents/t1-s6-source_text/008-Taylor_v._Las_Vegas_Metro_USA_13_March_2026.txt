13 I.      I GRANT IN PART TAYLOR’S MOTION FOR LEAVE TO FILE.

14         Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 15 requires district courts to “grant leave to amend freely

15 when justice so requires.” Owens v. Kaiser Found. Health Plan, Inc., 244 F.3d 708, 712 (9th Cir.

16 2001) (simplified). I apply this policy with “extreme liberality.” Id. (quotation omitted). I take

17 five factors into account “to assess the propriety of a motion for leave to amend: bad faith, undue

18 delay, prejudice to the opposing party, futility of amendment, and whether the plaintiff has

19 previously amended the complaint.” Johnson v. Buckley, 356 F.3d 1067, 1077 (9th Cir. 2004).

 8          The amended complaint must be a complete document in and of itself and will supersede

 9 the FAC in its entirety. Ramirez v. Cnty. of San Bernardino, 806 F.3d 1002, 1008 (9th Cir.

10 2015). Any allegations, parties, or requests for relief from prior papers that are not carried

11 forward in the second amended complaint will no longer be before the court. The second

12 amended complaint should identify the basis for the jurisdiction and venue of this court and

13 identify each party.

14          I advise Taylor to support each claim with factual allegations because all complaints

15 “must contain sufficient allegations of underlying facts to give fair notice and to enable the

16 opposing party to defend itself effectively.” Starr v. Baca, 652 F.3d 1202, 1216 (9th Cir.

17 2011). When claims are alleged against multiple defendants, the complaint should clearly

18 indicate which claims apply to which defendant. McHenry v. Renne, 84 F.3d 1172, 1178 (9th

19 Cir. 1996). The plaintiff should specifically identify each defendant and support each claim with

20 factual allegations about each defendant’s actions. Where multiple claims are alleged, the

21 complaint should identify which factual allegations give rise to each particular claim. Taylor

 6 II.         I DENY CLARK COUNTY AND UMC’S MOTION TO DISMISS.

 7             Clark County and UMC argue that all of Taylor’s claims must be dismissed because they

 8 sound in professional negligence and therefore require a medical expert affidavit under

 9 NRS § 41A.071. 5 NRS § 41A.071 does not apply in federal court because it is displaced by

10 Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 8, which requires only “a short and plain statement of the claim”

11 to file a complaint. See Berk v. Choy, 607 U.S. ----, 146 S. Ct. 546, 556-57 (2026). I therefore

12 deny Clark County and UMC’s motion to dismiss.

17 But I encourage Taylor to review the arguments the defendants make in those motions when
